Confidential governance module

Your wallet and participation are public; your choice is private.

A host-neutral confidential ballot core for Safe and OpenZeppelin Governor. Individual choices and exact totals never become public. Only the verdict is published — and only when enough wallets took part.

Tally

The problem

A public tally changes the vote it is counting.

Every open governance forum publishes running totals while voting is still open. That is not a neutral act. The count stops measuring preference and starts producing it.

  • 01

    Momentum

    Early totals tell later voters which side is already winning. Agreement gets cheaper than judgement.

  • 02

    Timing power

    A large holder who waits until the final block votes with strictly more information than everyone who went first.

  • 03

    Exposure

    A choice attached to a wallet is a choice attached to a person, an employer, and a counterparty who can read it forever.

The lifecycle

Five stages. One of them is unreadable.

What becomes public at each step: your wallet, your voting weight, and the time you acted. Your choice is not among them, at any stage.

  1. 01
    Preparing encrypted handle

    The Handle Gateway prepares the input and returns a handle the module can store.

  2. 02
    Ready for wallet

    The operation is assembled and waiting for you to sign it.

  3. 03
    Submitting

    The transaction goes on-chain. Your wallet and the time are now public.

  4. 04
    Confidential computation

    The Nox TEE stack folds the operation into the sealed totals. No progress figure is shown, because none is knowable.

  5. 05
    Recorded

    A receipt confirms the operation status — not a plaintext choice.

Committed rules

Fixed when the record opens. Unchangeable afterwards.

The privacy floor and the governance quorum are different rules and are never merged. The floor decides whether any result may be disclosed at all. The quorum decides whether the proposal passes.

Two outcomes

Only one of these ever reaches the public record.

if recorded wallets ≥ privacy floor

The verdict is published. Nothing else is.

Passed or Rejected — never the choices, never the exact totals. A Passed verdict authorizes the one action committed when the record opened, and no other.

Passed → 1,000 USDC to 0xD0c3…3e91

if recorded wallets < privacy floor

Result withheld.

Below the floor, too few wallets took part for any disclosure to be safe, so there is none. No verdict, no totals, no manual release, and nothing executes. This is a designed outcome, not a failure.

Stated limits

What this does not promise.

Several of these are achievable, and some are ahead of us. None of them are what the module does today, so none of them are claimed today.

Client-side encryption

The Handle Gateway prepares the input and sees it. Nothing is encrypted in your browser, and we do not say it is.

Anonymity

Your wallet, your weight, and the time you acted are all on-chain and linkable. Only the choice is withheld.

Receipt-freeness

Nothing here stops you from proving to someone else how you voted, if you decide to.

Bribe resistance

Replacement voting raises the cost of buying a vote, because the last accepted operation is the one that counts. It does not eliminate it.

A full tally proof

The evidence shows the configured Gateway signed a plaintext for the stored expected handle. That is a signature, not a proof of the computation.

Trust boundary

Who you are trusting, named.

A confidentiality claim is only as good as the list of parties who could break it. Here is ours, in full.

Trusted today

  • 01The Handle Gateway, which sees the input before it is sealed.
  • 02The Nox TEE stack that performs the confidential computation.
  • 03A single-node KMS holding the decryption key.
  • 04The Gateway signer whose signature the evidence rests on.
  • 05The deployed module code, which binds proposal, type, and replay.

What the proof actually checks

  • 01That the configured Gateway signed this plaintext for the stored expected handle.
  • 02That module state binds the proposal, the operation type, and replay protection.
The limitation we will not bury

The released Nox stack uses one KMS node holding the full private key. That is weaker than a threshold committee, where no single operator can decrypt alone. It belongs here, not in a footnote.
