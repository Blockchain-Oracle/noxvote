---
title: Honest limits
description: What NoxVote deliberately does not promise, and who you are trusting, named in full.
---

Several of these are achievable, and some are ahead of us. None of them are what the module
does today, so none of them are claimed today.

## Not promised

- **Client-side encryption.** The released SDK sends your encoded choice to iExec's attested
  Handle Gateway, which encrypts it and returns the handle. The Gateway sees the input during
  preparation. Nothing is encrypted in your browser, and we do not say it is.
- **Anonymity.** Your wallet, your weight, your participation, and your replacement timing
  are all on-chain and linkable. Only the choice is withheld.
- **Receipt-freeness.** Nothing here stops you from proving to someone else how you voted, if
  you decide to. MACI prevents convincing receipts through a hidden authenticated key/command
  state; released Nox does not supply that mechanism.
- **Bribe resistance.** Replacement voting raises the cost of buying a vote — the last
  accepted operation counts — but a coercer can still supervise the final action.
- **A full tally proof.** The accepted evidence is a Gateway signature over a decryption, not
  a proof of the computation or the complete private operation history.
- **Threshold key custody.** The released Nox stack uses **one KMS node holding the full
  private key**. That is weaker than a threshold committee, where no single operator can
  decrypt alone. It belongs here, not in a footnote.

## Trusted today, named

1. The Handle Gateway, which sees the input before it is sealed.
2. The Nox TEE stack that performs the confidential computation.
3. The single-node KMS holding the decryption key.
4. The Gateway signer whose signature the evidence rests on.
5. The deployed module code, which binds proposal, type, and replay.

## What still holds

Within that boundary, the load-bearing property survives: individual choices and exact totals
never appear on-chain, are never published afterwards, and are never opened per-ballot at the
end — unlike shielded-voting designs that reveal every ballot after close. Disclosure is one
boolean, gated by a privacy floor, bound to one committed action.
