title: Verify a result
description: What the decryption proof actually checks, what module state binds, and what you can independently inspect on-chain.
---

A confidentiality claim is only as good as what you can check. Here is exactly what the
evidence proves — and what it does not.

## What the proof checks

Finalization accepts one piece of evidence: the configured Gateway signer's EIP-712 signature
over the **exact verdict handle stored for that proposal** and its decrypted boolean result
(domain: `NoxCompute`, the chain ID, the NoxCompute contract). The core validates:

- the signature recovers to the configured Gateway signer;
- the handle is the one expected verdict handle stored at tally request — no other handle is
  accepted;
- the decrypted payload is a canonical boolean (`0x00`/`0x01`, exactly one byte);
- the chain domain matches — foreign-chain and foreign-host evidence rejects;
- finalization happens exactly once.

Short, mutated, wrong-signer, wrong-domain, wrong-handle, malformed-length, and
non-canonical-boolean evidence each reject independently, leaving the state unchanged.

## What module state binds

The proposal's committed action hash, dates, snapshot, quorum, privacy floor, and replacement
rule are immutable from registration. The verdict handle is derived from the ballot's
domain-separated ID, so identical encrypted inputs on two proposals still produce different
expected verdict handles — cross-proposal and cross-host replay cannot work.

## What you can inspect on-chain

- **ACL evidence**: every submitted ballot handle remains non-public
  (`isPubliclyDecryptable == false`), no viewer grant is ever emitted, and exactly one handle
  — the verdict — is marked publicly decryptable, by the core, after close.
- **Participation**: recorded unique wallets and weight, against the committed floor and
  quorum.
- **Execution match**: the executed Safe/Governor action recomputes to the registered action
  hash. Anything altered rejects.

## What the proof is not

It is a Gateway signature over a decryption, **not** a SNARK over the tally arithmetic or the
full private operation history. Proposal binding, expected type, and execute-once behavior
come from module state; trust in the computation itself rests on the parties named in the
trust boundary. Read [Honest limits](/docs/limits) — that page is part of this one.
